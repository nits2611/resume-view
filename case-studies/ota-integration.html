Case Study • Hospitality SaaS

Reduced OTA Onboarding from Months to Days

A real-world engineering story about identifying repeated integration effort, standardizing the core workflow, and reducing onboarding complexity without exposing proprietary business logic.

Context

At eZee Technosys, I worked on a hospitality SaaS channel manager platform connected with many Online Travel Agencies. The system handled important operations such as inventory updates, rate synchronization, reservations, guest information, booking details, and payment-related flows.

When a new OTA needed to be onboarded, the process was technically correct but operationally slow. It often required repeated development, review, testing, and third-party coordination.

Challenge

Business Pain

  • Long onboarding timelines affected client satisfaction.
  • Delayed partner integrations could slow client go-live.
  • Engineering resources were repeatedly pulled into similar work.

Technical Constraints

  • Legacy production system with low tolerance for disruption.
  • Each OTA had different formats and authentication patterns.
  • Quality checks could not be ignored or bypassed.

Approach

Mapped the full onboarding lifecycle

I reviewed the end-to-end flow, including requirement gathering, third-party API coordination, development, review, QA, and release readiness.

Separated business operations from format differences

The core OTA operations were mostly similar, but request/response formats and data structures varied. That difference became the main target for improvement.

Designed a reusable integration layer

The solution focused on configurable mappings and transformation logic so repeated custom development could be reduced for future integrations.

Balanced speed with stability

The goal was not to skip quality. The goal was to reduce repeated engineering effort while keeping validation, testing, and production safety intact.

Before vs After

Before

  • Repeated custom development for each OTA.
  • Long dependency on engineering and review cycles.
  • Client onboarding delays and frustration risk.
  • Higher chance of introducing bugs in repeated changes.

After

  • Reusable approach for common integration patterns.
  • Faster onboarding path for many OTA integrations.
  • Reduced repeated development workload.
  • Better stability by limiting unnecessary code changes.

Outcome

The approach helped reduce new OTA onboarding time from a typical 4–6 month cycle to under a week in many cases, with some integrations possible within days depending on complexity and validation needs.

The bigger value was not only speed. It improved engineering efficiency, reduced repeated work, supported faster client go-live, and showed how workflow-level thinking can create stronger business impact than simply writing more code.

Key Learnings

Fix the right problem

The original process was not wrong. It was safe but slow. The real opportunity was reducing repeatable effort.

Protect confidentiality

I can explain the approach and decision-making without exposing proprietary algorithms, internal data, or company-specific implementation details.

Think beyond code

Strong engineering impact often comes from improving systems, workflows, and decision quality.

Balance risk and speed

Fast delivery matters, but production stability and validation remain non-negotiable.
